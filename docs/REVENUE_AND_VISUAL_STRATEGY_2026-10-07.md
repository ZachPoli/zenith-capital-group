# Revenue and visual-assistant strategy — October 7, 2026

Status: recommendation for founder decision, not an approved change to the flagship priority or authorization to buy hardware. The founder wants faster revenue and is most enthusiastic about visual assistance. Income target, deadline, available hours and exact glasses model remain to be confirmed.

## Recommendation

Use a bounded paid-service offer for near-term cash while validating one visual workflow. Preserve FamiliarVoice's current users and finish a bounded release decision; do not let an indefinite polish cycle consume all available time. Avoid starting another broad consumer application merely because it is novel.

The existing Operations Automation Sprint is the clearest available short route to an invoice: one defined workflow, $750–$1,500 fixed fee, 50% upfront, explicit acceptance and limited support. This is an execution hypothesis, not a revenue guarantee; the old pipeline does not prove current buyer demand. Requalify old contacts before assuming an opportunity exists.

## Smart-glasses findings

Meta's current official FAQ says Device Access Toolkit 1.0 began rolling out September 30, 2026 and supports Ray-Ban Meta Gen 1/2 among other devices. It exposes camera/audio capabilities through mobile apps. This changes the assumption that Meta glasses are closed to useful development. It does not mean arbitrary on-glasses software or unrestricted access. Confirm model, firmware, country, developer access and the required end-to-end behavior on the owner's hardware.

The same FAQ still describes submission/discovery surfaces as forthcoming. Technical development access is not proof of frictionless commercial distribution. Verify the actual release path and acceptable-use terms before promising a glasses-only product.

Mentra's official support pages list Mentra Live at $449 and describe development on retail units, with Miniapp and standalone Bluetooth SDK paths. Treat it as a fallback to evaluate only after a specific Meta limitation blocks a validated workflow. Do not purchase hardware to answer a customer-demand question.

EssilorLuxottica reported more than seven million AI-glasses units sold in FY2025. That establishes meaningful hardware adoption, not Zenith's addressable paying audience: shipments are not active users, compatible users, reachable prospects or subscription buyers.

Meta already offers Live AI, and Be My Eyes provides hands-free volunteer access on Meta glasses. A generic “describe what I see” assistant faces existing alternatives. Zenith needs evidence that its chosen workflow offers a better repeatable outcome.

## Candidate offers to test

These are hypotheses, not validated markets or unique inventions. Compare prospects' existing software and methods before building.

| Candidate | Buyer and result | First test | Main uncertainty |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Existing automation sprint | Local industrial operator; remove recurring reporting/data-entry work | Observe a real workflow, quote one bounded improvement | Access to decision-maker and willingness to pay |
| Photo/voice-to-service-report | Small equipment service or maintenance business; turn job photos and dictated notes into its report template | Manually assist five reports on a phone, then offer a paid pilot | Whether savings exceed correction/review effort and existing tools already suffice |
| Visual receiving assistant | Small stockroom/distributor; capture labels, quantities and discrepancies into a reviewed CSV | One supplier format, one receiving task, human confirmation | OCR accuracy, integration burden and repeat frequency |
| Accessible task-specific visual companion | Blind/low-vision users; one difficult recurring task beyond current tools | Co-design with three users and compare against their current solution | Differentiation, safe reliability, payer and affordability |
| Desktop visual workflow coach | Users of one difficult business application; explain a screen and guide one task | One workflow using screen sharing, without automatic execution | Competition, support load and ability to acquire customers |

Best bridge between founder interest and possible revenue: **photo/voice-to-service-report**, initially phone-based and optionally glasses-enabled. It connects visual input to an output a business already needs. A buyer can judge time saved and report quality. Do not begin with general troubleshooting, autonomous repairs or navigation claims.

## A bounded 30-day experiment

Suggested allocation, subject to founder decision: 50% customer discovery/sales and paid delivery, 35% FamiliarVoice release blockers/support, 15% visual feasibility. Keep only one exploratory visual workflow active.

### Week 1: identify a buyer and prove existing hardware access

- Approach ten qualified contacts through founder-authorized outreach; aim for five conversations about the last time they performed the task, current costs and purchasing authority. No outreach is authorized by this document itself.
- Request a sanitized example report or workflow, with permission to use it for the pilot.
- Cap Meta feasibility at two working days: official sample, capture/frame access, audio reply, latency, reconnect behavior and distribution restrictions. Use the phone camera if hardware access blocks the customer test.
- Reconcile FamiliarVoice's current release checklist without treating open engineering work as shipped.

### Week 2: sell a specific result

- Offer a scoped $750–$1,500 automation sprint or a proposed $300–$750 report-workflow pilot. The latter is an experimental quote range, not a market benchmark.
- Define number of jobs/reports, inputs, output format, review responsibility, delivery date and support boundary before taking a deposit.
- Seek one paid pilot or deposit before substantial new product engineering. Verbal enthusiasm alone does not satisfy this gate.

### Weeks 3–4: deliver and measure

- Deliver one workflow. Compare baseline time, correction time, output quality and actual repeated use.
- Seek a second paying buyer with substantially the same need. Only then decide whether reusable software warrants a separate active product track.
- If ten qualified conversations and three concrete proposals yield no payment, change the buyer/problem/offer before expanding features or buying hardware. This is a suggested stop rule, not statistically conclusive market research.
- For FamiliarVoice, review remaining launch gates, observed repeat use and willingness to pay. Decide explicitly whether to continue monetization, maintain a limited beta, or time-box remaining work; protect existing commitments.

## Revenue arithmetic, not a forecast

One $1,000 project with 50% upfront produces a $500 initial payment; the remainder depends on delivery/acceptance. Three such completed projects produce $3,000 gross revenue before labor, selling time, costs and taxes. Capacity and sales conversion remain unknown.

At a hypothetical $15/month, 200 subscribers produce $3,000 monthly gross revenue. At $49/month, 62 business accounts produce $3,038. Neither example establishes demand, profit or likely acquisition cost. Calculate contribution after inference, platform/payment fees, support and refunds before scaling.
