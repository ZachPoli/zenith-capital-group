# Zenith Capital Group — First Paid Offer

## Offer Name

**Operations Automation Sprint**

## Who It Is For

Small manufacturers, machine shops, fabricators, warehouses, distributors, and industrial service businesses with one repetitive operational workflow that is taking more manual effort than it should.

Typical examples:
- Re-entering the same information into multiple spreadsheets or systems.
- Manually building recurring reports.
- Poor visibility into materials, inventory, job status, service work, or production information.
- Repetitive CSV cleanup or spreadsheet manipulation.
- Shop-to-office or field-to-office information handoffs that rely on email, paper, or copy/paste.

The sprint is not sold until discovery confirms a real problem worth fixing.

## Customer Promise

Zenith will take **one clearly defined manual workflow** and build the smallest practical improvement that reduces repetitive work, errors, or information friction.

The customer is buying an operational result, not a programming language, dashboard, database, or AI feature.

## Standard Sprint

**Target price: $750–$1,500 fixed fee**

Use the lower end for a tightly scoped first engagement. Use the upper end when the workflow has more data, users, integrations, or operational risk.

**Internal floor for an unusually valuable first-customer proof project: $500.** Do not advertise this as the standard price and do not automatically discount to it.

### Payment

- 50% to start.
- 50% on delivery/acceptance.
- For a very small $500 engagement, payment can be 100% upfront if the scope is simple and explicit.

## What Is Included

1. **Workflow definition**
   - Current process.
   - Who performs it.
   - Frequency.
   - Inputs and outputs.
   - Current tools.
   - Main source of wasted time, errors, delays, or poor visibility.

2. **One scoped operational improvement**
   Examples may include:
   - Spreadsheet automation.
   - Data cleanup/import/export workflow.
   - Inventory or material-tracking tool.
   - Recurring operational report.
   - Lightweight dashboard.
   - Small internal web/desktop tool.
   - Simple workflow automation between existing files/systems.

3. **Delivery and handoff**
   - Working solution.
   - Basic setup/deployment appropriate to the project.
   - Short written operating instructions.
   - One handoff/review session.

4. **Post-delivery correction period**
   - 14 days for defects or behavior that does not match the agreed scope.
   - New features or changed requirements are separate work.

## Scope Rule

A sprint solves **one workflow**.

If discovery reveals several unrelated problems, choose the highest-value problem and make the others future opportunities.

Do not turn a $750–$1,500 sprint into an ERP replacement, full custom business platform, company-wide data migration, or indefinite consulting engagement.

## What Zenith Does Not Promise

- A predetermined technology stack.
- A full replacement for the customer's ERP/MRP/accounting system.
- Unlimited revisions.
- Unlimited support.
- Guaranteed savings before the current process has been measured.
- Complex integrations with third-party systems before access, API capability, security constraints, and ownership are verified.

## Discovery Qualification

Before quoting, establish:

1. What exactly happens today?
2. Who does it?
3. How often?
4. Roughly how much time does it consume?
5. What errors, delays, rework, or visibility problems does it create?
6. What tools/files/systems are involved?
7. What would a useful improvement look like?
8. Who owns the decision to approve and pay for the work?

A useful signal is a specific statement such as:

> “Someone spends four hours every Friday doing this manually.”

That is stronger than a vague request for “better software.”

## Proposal Transition

When a real problem is identified:

> “I think there may be a small, practical way to improve that without replacing the systems you already use. Let me scope the simplest version that could produce a useful result, and I’ll send you a fixed-price proposal.”

Do not quote on the spot unless the scope is exceptionally simple and clear.

## Pricing Logic

Price based on the value, risk, and complexity of the workflow—not the number of lines of code.

### Rough internal bands

- **$500–$750:** very small proof project; one user; simple spreadsheet/data automation; low deployment risk.
- **$750–$1,500:** standard first sprint; one workflow; modest data/tooling complexity; clear operational deliverable.
- **$1,500–$3,000+:** multiple users, more complex deployment, multiple data sources, significant migration, permissions, or integration work. At this point, confirm whether the project is still truly a sprint.

If the problem is larger than these bands, scope a paid discovery/assessment or break the work into phases rather than giving away a large custom build under a small fixed fee.

## First-Customer Goal

The first engagement should optimize for:

1. A real customer paying real money.
2. A problem Zenith can solve reliably.
3. A short path to delivery.
4. A measurable before/after result.
5. Permission to request a testimonial and referral after successful delivery.

The first customer does not need to maximize revenue. The first customer needs to prove that Zenith can turn a real operational pain into a paid result.

## After a Successful Sprint

Only after the customer sees value, explore:

- Additional workflows.
- Ongoing support/maintenance.
- Recurring reporting or monitoring.
- A larger internal tool.
- A monthly retainer.
- Referral to another local business.

Do not lead with recurring revenue before the first useful result is delivered.
