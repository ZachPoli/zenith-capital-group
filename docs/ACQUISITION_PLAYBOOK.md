# Zenith Capital Group — Acquisition Playbook

## Purpose
Build the capability to buy profitable businesses without turning an acquisition into a personal financial emergency or another owner-dependent job.

## Acquisition Thesis — Version 1
Prefer small, understandable businesses where Zenith can create value through software, automation, data, process improvement, customer acquisition, or better management systems.

### Categories worth studying
- Micro-SaaS and niche B2B software
- Small software agencies with recurring revenue
- Managed IT / technology services
- Data, reporting, and workflow-automation businesses
- Technology-enabled logistics and operations services
- Boring local/service businesses with clear opportunities for software and automation

## Readiness Gate
Do not buy simply because a deal is available. Before making a serious offer, answer yes to most of these:

- [ ] Personal emergency runway is protected.
- [ ] Zenith's own books are clean and current.
- [ ] The founder can manage people and systems without doing every task personally.
- [ ] The acquisition has an understandable path to debt service and owner compensation.
- [ ] Capital structure is clear before signing.
- [ ] Accountant and attorney are available for diligence/closing.
- [ ] There is a written 100-day operating plan.
- [ ] A failed acquisition would not threaten the founder's home or basic survival.

## Search Funnel

### 1. Source
- Brokers
- Direct owner outreach
- Industry contacts
- Accountants / attorneys
- Online marketplaces
- Local business networks

### 2. Initial Screen
Collect:
- Asking price
- Revenue
- Seller's discretionary earnings / normalized operating profit
- Revenue trend
- Customer concentration
- Recurring vs project revenue
- Owner hours/week
- Employee/contractor dependencies
- Reason for sale
- Required capex
- Existing debt

### 3. Quality Screen
Ask:
- Would customers notice if the owner disappeared for 30 days?
- Is revenue transferable to a new owner?
- Does any single customer represent dangerous concentration?
- Are margins real after replacing the owner's labor?
- Does the company own the software/IP it claims to own?
- Is the technology maintainable?
- Are churn and customer acquisition economics understandable?

### 4. Diligence
Verify rather than accept summaries.

Financial:
- Tax returns
- Bank statements
- P&Ls and balance sheets
- Payroll
- Accounts receivable/payable
- Debt
- Owner add-backs

Commercial:
- Customer contracts
- Revenue concentration
- Churn / retention
- Sales pipeline
- Pricing
- Competitors

Technical (for software businesses):
- Source-code ownership
- Repositories and access controls
- Licenses / third-party dependencies
- Hosting costs
- Security posture
- Technical debt
- Deployment process
- Key-person dependency

Legal / operational:
- Entity documents
- Employee/contractor agreements
- Litigation / claims
- Vendor contracts
- Insurance
- Licenses
- Transferability of key agreements

## Financing Toolbox to Learn
- Buyer equity
- Seller financing
- Earnouts where appropriate
- Conventional bank debt
- SBA-backed acquisition financing when eligible
- Outside equity only when incentives and control are understood

Never assume financing terms until a real lender or qualified advisor confirms them.

## First 100 Days After an Acquisition

### Days 1–30: Observe
- Preserve customer relationships.
- Meet every key employee.
- Understand cash conversion and working capital.
- Change only urgent problems.

### Days 31–60: Systematize
- Management dashboard
- SOPs
- Sales pipeline visibility
- Cash forecast
- Customer retention metrics

### Days 61–100: Improve
- Automate repetitive work.
- Improve pricing where evidence supports it.
- Eliminate waste.
- Clarify roles/accountability.
- Begin technology improvements with measurable ROI.

## Deal-Killer List
Walk away or heavily re-price when:
- Financials cannot be verified.
- Source-code/IP ownership is ambiguous.
- The seller is the entire company.
- Customer concentration creates existential risk.
- The deal requires nearly all personal liquidity.
- Debt service depends on aggressive growth assumptions.
- The seller resists reasonable diligence.
- The business looks cheap only because a major reinvestment is being ignored.

## Principle
The objective is not to become someone who "buys businesses." The objective is to become a disciplined capital allocator who buys only when the expected return justifies the risk and Zenith has the operational ability to make the asset better.
